## What's going on

- **A trend, not a result.** It starts from a saying about coincidences becoming a pattern and
  uses last weekend's late winner as the third instance.
- **The delayed reveal.** The record for substitute winning goals is introduced without naming
  who holds it; a couple of paragraphs later it turns out to be Arsenal themselves. "Funnily enough"
  flags the twist and the piece pivots to the longer pattern.
- **Layering from narrow to wide:** this season → the record → the Arteta era → caveats
  (squad depth, tight games) → timing of changes → all-time Premier League comparison. The
  caveats make the final claim more believable.
- **Charts placed after their claims,** three of them, so the data lands before the argument
  moves on.
- **A light, forward-looking last line** (an idiom tying the bench to the title race) rather
  than a recap.

## Steal-able

- The withheld fact: introduce the record, hold back who owns it, then reveal.
- Zoom out in layers: this week, this season, this era, all time.
- Admit the caveats before the conclusion.
