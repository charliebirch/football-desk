## What Charlie said

- Don't like this one, it's too on the nose with trying to be funny and clever. Too many
  analogies or catchphrases throughout. Stopped reading half way through.

## What's going on

- **Density of jokes.** Around 25 comic devices in about 1,000 words: pop-culture and
  sitcom references, a sketch-show catchphrase, invented coaching lore, nickname puns, mythic
  imagery and a run of absurd similes. Most of the pop-culture ones sit in the first half,
  which is where Charlie gave up.
- **The headline is a joke too.** A coined phrase echoing "Total Football" plus a made-up word,
  meant ironically. The tone is set as clever before the reader knows what happened.
- **Facts become background.** The record and the stats are slipped in as asides for the
  jokes to play with, rather than doing the work.

## Steal-able (what to avoid)

- Humour that announces itself. Compare #9 (one quiet image) and #12 (a bracketed aside):
  Charlie likes those. The difference is frequency and whether the joke serves the point.
- A working rule to test: at most one clear joke or image per section, and it has to say
  something.
