## What's going on

- **The opening sets ordinary words against an extraordinary situation.** It starts with a
  City post-match quote about character and togetherness, points out those lines could follow
  any setback (an injury crisis, a sacking), then says what the real context is: a guilty
  verdict and no idea what comes next. The reader feels how inadequate the stock phrases are
  before the argument starts.
- **The questions come late, as a concession.** The two rhetorical questions sit in the
  second-last paragraph: after calling the manager's "just win" message absurd, Wilson asks
  what else players and managers could possibly do. The questions soften his own attack and
  set up the ending.
- **The ending is a big statement that closes the loop.** It returns to the opening's theme,
  language, and scales it up: this is a crisis unlike anything English football has known, and
  football doesn't have the words for it. Charlie's "argot" is most likely in that line, so the
  sentence he loved also contains the word he disliked.
- **The middle is argument with numbers.** City's net spend over nine years (about 50% more
  than anyone else, before wages) is used to knock down the club's defence. About 620 words.
- **One embedded link** to the paper's earlier coverage of the findings, placed on the phrase
  it supports.

## Steal-able

- Open with the stock phrase everyone uses, then show why it doesn't fit this time.
- End by returning to the opening's idea, bigger. Say it in plain words: the big statement
  doesn't need a rare word to land.
