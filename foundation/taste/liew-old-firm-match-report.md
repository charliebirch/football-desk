## What's going on

- **It's mostly not play-by-play.** About three-quarters of Liew's ~850 words are context: the
  silent stadium, the derby's toxicity, Celtic's three defeats, O'Neill's future, questions
  about players and the board. The match action is concentrated near the end and the result
  arrives almost in passing. Charlie liked exactly the part that isn't a report.
- **Opens on a scene,** the silence after the goal, so the atmosphere carries the idea before
  any detail.
- **Imagery and pointed questions** (Celtic as a drawer of tangled cables; a run of questions
  about named players) do the analysis.
- **His question is a real one.** For a reader who has seen the highlights, the match itself
  is the least valuable part. What's left to sell is meaning, context and judgement.

## Steal-able

- If you ever write after a match: lead with what it meant, not what happened.
- From Australia this matters doubly: your readers wake up to the result. Your value is the
  "so what".
