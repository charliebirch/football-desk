## What's going on

- **Opening in the middle of things.** It starts as if the conversation is already under way
  ("And yet...") on the Anfield match, then uses fragments ("Up. Down.") to show the confusion
  before naming it "unsustainable". The questions that drive it ("will the result even
  stand?") come a few paragraphs in.
- **Rhythm.** Very short punchy lines alternate with long clause-heavy ones, and paragraphs
  often end on a short weighted line. That's a lot of why it feels gripping.
- **The ending widens the frame.** It returns to the opening's uncertainty and scales it up
  from one match to the whole season. Charlie's "big statement".
- **Vocabulary he rejected.** Elevated or specialist words (a French phrase, "neoliberal",
  "galvanised", rule-book jargon) give it a commentary tone but cost accessibility. This is a
  clear, stated line for his own style.
- **Links as a web.** Embedded links to the outlet's earlier coverage let readers go deeper
  without the piece explaining everything.

## Steal-able

- Start in the middle of the moment, then state the thesis once the reader is hooked.
- End by zooming out: the same idea as the opening, at a bigger scale.
- Plain-words rule: if a word needs a dictionary, swap it.
