## 2. Charlie's vocabulary

The words the style guide and future feedback should use:

> fact backed · gets to the point · not click baity · "the feel for the read" · subtle
> cleverness · too on the nose · punchy · snappy · not too verbose · a good length ·
> consumable · the layers · the reveal · poignant, sharp and big statement · accessible
> language · makes you think and question the system · the feeling I get from this writer ·
> cheap comments · rookie · could have been written by AI

## 3. What Charlie doesn't want to do

- Humour that announces itself: "too on the nose with trying to be funny and clever. Too many
  analogies or catchphrases" (#7)
- Jokes leaned on instead of the story: "tell the story dont just use analogies and jokes over
  and over" (#14, Q3). A line that would land in a strong piece reads as cheap in a weak one
- Words readers may not know (#4)
- Misleading or clickbait headlines (#1)
- Play-by-play match write-ups (#8)
- Stock lines that could be in anyone's piece; "could have been written by AI" (#14)
- Sponsorship slipped into the writing (#14)
- Sloppy, broken-looking presentation (#14, #15)
- Very long pieces, for now (#3, #13)

## 5. Forms that suit Charlie

On this evidence, the forms to practise first:

1. **The data-led analysis piece, 600-900 words** (#10, #11): a trend or idea, proved with
   numbers, zoomed out in layers.
2. **The opinion or human-angle column, 600-900 words** (#2, #9): a clear stance, one person
   or event standing for something bigger.
3. **A short recurring series** (#12): a fixed shape every week, e.g. knee-jerk reactions from
   the Australian morning-after seat.

Not yet: match reports, long reads, access-heavy reported features.

**Working hypothesis for Charlie's lane:** data-backed, plain-spoken, a bit of wit, from the
far-away seat. (To confirm: question 1.)

## 9. Drills that follow

One per technique group, 200-500 words each. `practice` sets them properly once Charlie has
picked his techniques.

1. **Same story twice:** a 250-word report and a 250-word column on one real story, each with a
   headline that sets its feel (C1, C2).
2. **The trend piece:** one stat trend from this weekend, claim-then-proof, three layers out, one
   withheld reveal (C3, C4, C5).
3. **Five openings** for one piece, then pick the best and say why (C7).
4. **Three knee-jerks** from this weekend: verdict heading, data, caveat, one bracketed aside
   (C6, C12).
5. **One moment, one image:** 300 words on a single moment, exactly one comparison (C6).
6. **Plain words:** rewrite a dense paragraph from #4 so anyone could follow it (C11).
