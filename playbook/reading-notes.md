## Craft picks (question 6)

The techniques your notes point to. Tick the ones you want on your craft list, and add a line in your own words under any you'd put differently. Numbers in brackets are the pieces above.

- [ ] **C1 Honest headline.** The title tells you what the piece is and how it'll feel (#1, #2)
- [ ] **C2 Report or column, decided first,** and kept to all the way through (#1, #2, #13)
- [ ] **C3 Claim, then proof.** The stat or chart comes straight after the sentence it backs up (#6, #10, #11)
- [ ] **C4 Zoom out in layers.** This week, this season, this era, all time (#11)
- [ ] **C5 The delayed reveal.** Introduce the record, hold back who owns it (#11, Arsenal)
- [ ] **C6 One image, not a stream of jokes.** Tell the story; one well-placed line lands, ten don't (#9's M3 line, #12's "everyone (not us)" vs #7 and #14)
- [ ] **C7 A gripping opening.** Start in the middle of the moment, or on a stock phrase that doesn't fit this time (#4, #9)
- [ ] **C8 End wide.** One sharp line that returns to the opening idea, bigger (#4, #11)
- [ ] **C9 Name the idea.** A short label that carries the argument (#10, "B club")
- [ ] **C10 Meaning over match.** The so-what, not the play-by-play (#8 vs #11)
- [ ] **C11 Plain words.** If it needs a dictionary, swap it (#4, "argot")
- [ ] **C12 Visible sections,** with something to look at between them (#12 headings, #13 dividers)
- [ ] **C13 Front-load, then a cliffhanger.** Best points first, free part ends on what's still to come; for Substack (#6)
- [ ] **C14 Simple, then the consequence.** Explain the complicated thing simply, then say what it means for the sport (#0, Business of Sport)
